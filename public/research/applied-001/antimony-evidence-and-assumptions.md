# U.S. Antimony Supply Resilience, 2027–2031

**Evidence, assumptions and calculation register**  
**Research cutoff / source retrieval:** 4 November 2026  
**Research foundation:** [Strategic Materials Policy Tracker — Antimony](https://strategic-materials-policy-tracker.vercel.app/materials/antimony)  
**Decision perspective:** Chief Procurement Officer, Chief Strategy Officer and CFO of a modeled U.S. defense manufacturer.

## 1. Executive analytical conclusion

The qualified, balanced sourcing policy remains the leading *illustrative* response: develop an independently traceable alternative import lane targeting 45% of annual volume, maintain a **provisional five-month reserve of buyer-qualified input**, and negotiate a **contingent 30% domestic offtake target** tied to delivered, usable material. The 30% figure is a future sourcing objective; the analysis assumes zero deliverable domestic volume from that target in 2027–2029 and 15% effective volume in 2030. The main annual cases assume the alternative 45% lane is **already qualified and deliverable**; a separate 2027 downside test removes that assumption.

Under the modeled 2030 eight-month severe interruption, five months of initial stock absorbs the full 39.00 t disrupted volume. At 100 t annual demand and the USGS 2025 metal price benchmark, five months of inventory costs **$2,296,482** and the sum of planned sourcing premiums, one-year inventory carry and the event's assumed spot markup is **$812,955**. This comparison uses assumptions, not actual supplier quotes, customer volumes or company accounts.

**Conditionality:** With the 45% alternative channel already qualified, the severe scenario before domestic qualification leaves **4.35 t** unfilled with five months of inventory. **If no alternative channel qualifies in 2027, 16.50 t remains unfilled** as modeled volumes fall back to the incumbent channel. If the alternative-source lane is curtailed 75% rather than 45%, the 2030 gap rises to **5.70 t**. A correlated domestic delay and 75% alternative curtailment raises it to **12.45 t**. The five-month policy is a procurement design to be tested, not an unconditional purchasing instruction. Reserve a sixth-month purchase option where supplier traceability or continuity criticality warrants it.

**Technical unit discipline.** The model expresses all demand, physical stocks and disrupted deliveries in **metric tons of contained Sb-equivalent**. Actual products include concentrate, metal, antimony trioxide and military-specified antimony trisulfide, with different Sb content, prices, quality requirements, production steps and storage properties. The USGS metal benchmark provides an **analytical cost proxy**; a purchasing model would replace it with prices, mass-balance conversions and qualified material specifications by SKU.

## 4. Financial and physical calculations

For each year / strategy / scenario:

1. **Effective domestic share** = target domestic share × qualified ramp fraction. The remaining target is reallocated to the incumbent lane. Alternative import share remains fixed as a procurement design assumption.
2. **Gross disrupted quantity (t)** = buyer annual demand × scenario months / 12 × sum of each lane's effective share × its scenario curtailed fraction.
3. **Beginning reserve (t)** = annual demand × stock months / 12. **Stock drawn (t)** = minimum of gross disruption and reserve.
4. **Emergency fill (t)** = uncovered disruption after stock × modeled emergency-fill fraction. **Residual unfilled (t)** = gross disruption − stock drawn − emergency fill.
5. **Planned annual sourcing premium** = demand × USGS metal price proxy per t × (alternative share × alternative premium + effective domestic share × domestic premium).
6. **Annual stock carry** = initial reserve × price proxy × annual carry rate. **Spot markup** = emergency fill × price proxy × emergency-price markup. **Modeled annual incremental expense** = planned sourcing premium + stock carry + spot markup.
7. **Initial cash tied up in inventory** = starting reserve × metal price proxy; shown separately from incremental expense. It is working capital and an inventory asset, distinct from the annual expense projection.

The model **does not assert full transaction-level cash outlays**: actual source quantities bought during an interrupted year, base-price principal on replacement procurement, stock replenishment, carrying costs for partial-year holdings, duty and freight, storage capacity, throughput and commercial losses need contract-specific data. Every annual event independently begins with the stated inventory. The model is an **episodic stress test**, not a five-year stock-flow forecast. No frequency-weighted expectation or NPV is calculated.

## 6. Material open questions and decision gates

**Procurement + Engineering:** bill of materials by SKU, contained-Sb conversion factors, qualification protocols, test acceptance, storage requirements and shelf life; refinery and smelter location; chain-of-custody, permitted end use and export-licensing legal advice; achievable substitute or redesign options; lead-time / pipeline inventory.

**Finance:** supplier quotes by form, grade and currency; freight, duties, financing rates, carrying cost and insured storage; actual cash cap; production interruption cost per day; replenishment and reverse logistics; customer backlog and penalty exposure.

**Strategy + Legal:** conditional Stibnite offtake options tied to final financing, project completion, processing agreements, qualified grade, guaranteed deliveries and enforceable remedies. Any domestic-production assumption is gated by documentary progress and accepted shipments.

**Decision reversal:** with zero qualified alternative access in 2027 the five-month case leaves 16.50 t uncovered; qualify the channel before relying on the policy. Raise buffer toward six months, accelerate a distinct refining route or renegotiate delivery options if the alternative source shares origin/refinery exposure, the input's 5 t tolerance is too high, the disruption lasts beyond the selected eight-month event, or the metal-equivalent procurement price breaches the working-capital ceiling. A live decision requires grade-specific contracts and inventory replenishment economics.
