## 1. Governing question and competing hypotheses

**Decision:** At what conditions should BHP continue project development, take a final investment decision (FID), accelerate available pre-FID work, or stage funding for the Escondida New Concentrator, given the need to replace Los Colorados and capital competing across its copper and broader portfolio?

The reported Goldman Sachs average-2027 copper forecast of US$15,400/t is a **near-term market forecast**. BHP's disclosed Escondida project IRR of 16–18% uses UBS May 2026 **long-term copper-price consensus of $4.76/lb**, or approximately **US$10,494/t**. Its published 230–270kt annual copper output is **production from the new facility**, not equivalent mine-wide incremental volume. Neither high 2027 prices nor simple multiplication of new-facility tonnage and copper price can establish a project NPV.

Test four hypotheses:

- H1 (liquidity): stronger realized near-term prices increase BHP operating flexibility and could help finance copper investments without needing to forecast project-life high prices.
- H2 (horizon): 2027 commodity calls have limited direct relevance to a concentrator scheduled to start in 2031–32; the long-term expected price path should dominate the IRR calculation.
- H3 (replacement and readiness): loss of Los Colorados capacity, grade trajectory, recoveries, processing throughput, permitting and schedule substantially affect preserve-versus-grow economics.
- H4 (portfolio): even a positive project return does not prove acceleration is optimal against capital expenditure on Copper SA, Jansen, other projects or debt/distributions.

**Provisional interpretation:** Continue development and preserve FID optionality; do not recommend accelerating construction or full FID merely because of a bullish 2027 price forecast. This is conditional, not an endorsement of the project's independent value.

## 3. Near-term copper market: three incompatible forecasting lenses

### Market price forecasts, **not interchangeable planning assumptions**

| Reference | Date and basis | 2027 USD/t | Confidence and use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| World Bank Commodity Markets Outlook | October 27/28 2026; forecast using data cutoff Oct 14 | **13,000** | **Primary independent publication**; base/credible external downside comparator |
| Goldman Sachs previously reported forecast | Before Oct 19 2026 research revision | **13,800** | Public secondary reporting; reference for *forecast revision*, not a contemporaneous source |
| Reported LME 2027 forward curve in Goldman account | October 19 2026 observation | **14,405** | **Secondary snapshot**; a price curve at a date, not institutional valuation consensus |
| Goldman Sachs revised average-2027 call | Oct 19 2026 research reported Oct 22–25 | **15,400** | Corroborated public secondary reporting but original authorized bank report not examined |
| BHP published project IRR assumption | May 2026 long-term UBS consensus | **10,494** | **Primary BHP disclosure of underlying *long-run* assumption**, not a competing 2027 market forecast |

World Bank also forecasts copper **US$13,200/t in 2026** and **US$12,500/t in 2028**, directly from its October 2026 published forecast table. World Bank assumes a partial correction from the recent record rally; its report discusses copper prices distorted by expected refined-metal tariff policy and inventory flows. Compare the **date and forecasting horizon** of each estimate before asserting that one institution is objectively right.

The Goldman-to-World Bank 2027 gap is **US$2,400/t**, or **18.46% relative to the World Bank forecast** (Goldman's forecast is ~15.58% above World Bank *measured relative to Goldman* if framed in reverse).

### Refined copper supply/demand accounting

International Copper Study Group (ICSG), October 14 2026 forecast:

- **Worldwide refined copper surplus** ~**+104kt in 2026**, **+368kt in 2027**; 2027 production about 29.67Mt, usage 29.30Mt, rounded.
- Reported Goldman forecast: **market outside United States** refined deficit **-950kt in 2026**, **-220kt in 2027**, partly linked to unusual US cathode stock accumulation.

These numbers **cannot be subtracted as though they represent the same supply/demand geography**. They may both be true because one excludes the United States and the other is worldwide; inventory accounting and differences in underlying production/usage forecasts also matter. ICSG official release directory confirms October 14 release; JOGMEC (government resource agency) October 23 report independently quotes the surplus figures. The ICSG primary PDF did not load in the research browser, so mark this **official-release-identified / government secondary confirmation**, not first-hand PDF verification.

### Tariffs and industrial-policy transmission

U.S. CBP guidance dated June 5, 2026 confirms additional Section 232 duties applied to defined **copper articles and derivatives** under separate HTSUS classifications. **Do not claim that a future broad refined-copper tariff had already entered into force on that basis**. The World Bank and Goldman reporting both identify *uncertainty about possible refined-copper tariffs* as influencing warehouse flows and basis spreads. FI002's geopolitics module should track the specific refined-vs-semifabricated product distinction, customs implementation dates and actual inventory build, and then explain the revenue price channel rather than assigning an unverified tariff profit.

## 4. Reproducible early quantitative scenarios — illustrative ONLY

Unit conversion: 1 metric tonne = **2,204.62262185 lb**. 1 US$/t = 100 / 2,204.62262185 cents/lb.

**BHP's static FY2026 copper EBITDA sensitivity:** `Δ EBITDA ($m) = (Δ price ($/t)/2204.62262185)*100*39`.

| 2027 copper price reference | Price US$/t | Delta vs older reported GS $13,800/t | BHP FY26-basis *hypothetical* delta underlying EBITDA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| World Bank Oct 2026 | 13,000 | -800 | **-US$1.415bn** |
| Previous reported Goldman call | 13,800 | 0 | **0** |
| Oct 19 forward snapshot (secondary) | 14,405 | +605 | **+US$1.070bn** |
| Revised reported Goldman call | 15,400 | +1,600 | **+US$2.830bn** |

The World Bank-vs-Goldman spread translates to **US$4.246bn** under the *same purely illustrative FY2026-basis calculation*. This is **not a probability-weighted earnings spread, company forecast, or FCF bridge**. The sensitivity is a local approximation for an existing-portfolio mix, and large shocks/production revisions may not behave linearly. Prices must eventually be linked to 2027 output, realized discounts, byproducts, cost responses, tax and actual cash realization. Avoid adding these hypothetical amounts to BHP guidance as though a fully priced FCF bridge has been constructed.

BHP's separate management forecasts of copper segment FCF: about **US$12bn for FY2027–31 and US$24bn for FY2032–35**, attributable basis, **after projected capex**, at its assumptions; the figures already include investment effects. Group capex forecast around **US$11bn/year** with ~$4bn for growth, over 55% growth copper focus (66% with certain non-operated JV investments). FY27 capex + exploration ~US$11bn, copper ~US$5.4bn and potash ~US$2.0bn. Avoid double-deducting new concentrator capex from management FCF or treating segment FCF as instantly accessible cash at group treasury without qualifying interest/tax and JV distributions.

**Modeling discipline:** Slide-40 footnote 1 states estimated capex **in real US dollars as of 1 July 2026** for the comparison projects, **except Escondida, Spence and Cerro Colorado in nominal US dollars**. Do not rank their cost estimates by simply adding/ranking nominal dollar amounts. Vicuña '100%' is a project scope notation, not proof BHP owns 100% of the venture. Project production capacities are not necessarily incremental. Also review BHP's other capital needs, including **FY2027 potash capex plus exploration US$2.0bn** (slide 50).

BHP's slide-19 five-year free cash flow forecast suggests approximately **US$35bn FY2027–31 at its simultaneous consensus copper ($5.40/lb), iron-ore ($91/t), and potash ($302/t) assumptions**, but its downside (~US$15bn) and spot (~US$50bn) cases move **several commodities together**. These cannot be isolated into a copper-only sensitivity without an additional company model. Their cash flows already net relevant capital expenditure.

The decision model must preserve the distinctions: **corporate affordability**, **project investment return**, **project schedule/readiness**, and **availability of another higher-return project** are four different questions.

## 5. Timing mismatch and genuine capital allocation

Current timing:

1. FY2026 audited results and sensitivity disclose historical resilience.
2. FY2027 Goldman forecast tests *near-term price exposure*; realized production/grade still risks declining. At FY27 Q1 BHP produced **401kt of copper**, down approximately **19%** year-over-year, while prices were high. This is evidence against a simplistic full price-to-volume cashflow translation.
3. Environmental questions / answers and engineering readiness determine whether CY2027–28 FID is feasible.
4. 2031–32 target production introduces multiple years of commodity-cycle uncertainty before first output.
5. For an eventual FID, BHP should test long-run price, recovery, replacement/continued capacity, capex, ramp-up/delay, permitting and competing portfolio uses against an appropriately specified return hurdle.

**Disclosures not yet sufficient for a defendable independent NPV/IRR:** granular pre- and post-Los Colorados production baseline (payable metal by circuit); quality/ore grades; annual capital spending curve; project cash costs, water/power costs; fiscal/royalty/tax by modeled year; post-FID shutdown schedule; incremental recoveries; salvage/terminal value; working capital and fiscal credit details; consolidation/free-cash-flow bridges; legally agreed JV capex contribution mechanism; commissioning success probabilities. No synthetic cash flows should be reverse-engineered merely to hit 16–18% IRR.

### Practical alternative evaluation

**A. Maintain development schedule / disciplined FID:** Advance engineering and permitted precommitment, retain BHP's 2027–28 FID target subject to published gating. Candidate baseline.

**B. Accelerate selective engineering and readiness, not full project investment:** Investigate vendor lead times, design, testing, right-of-way and procurement *within already authorized funding*; acceleration beyond approved scope requires identifiable incremental cost, permitting and readiness evidence.

**C. Stage FID / preserve optionality:** Delay irrevocable funding if returns fall below hurdle or permitting/design/cost remain unresolved, but explicitly model potential mine-wide throughput lost from retiring Los Colorados; deferral is not costless.

**D. Major deferral / do nothing:** Must be measured against a credible physically achievable replacement baseline, not imagined maintenance of Los Colorados indefinitely.

A sound future recommendation needs *trigger thresholds*: official environmental RCA; FID readiness; replacement-capacity bridge; total project cost range and escalation; independently defined long-run copper price and discount rate; grade/recovery performance; downside project returns; competing cash commitment stress.
