# SMPT — Four-Layer Analytical Framework & Applied Strategy Standard

**Status:** Standing research and publication standard  
**Adopted:** 5 November 2026  
**Applies to:** New SMPT Applied Strategy products and financial/industrial-strategy analyses, including presentation decks and accompanying spreadsheets. Existing published work is not retroactively changed by this document.

This document defines how SMPT turns its research corpus into **decision-oriented corporate analysis**. The tracker remains a source-linked comparative record of policy, capital and reported activity. Applied Strategy products are **separate, attributed analytical outputs**: they may use explicit assumptions, scenarios, sensitivities and recommendations, but must not insert inferred prices, forecasts, valuations or model outputs into the factual tracker as though they were source records.

**Operating thesis:** Industrial intelligence is the foundation; corporate strategy is the application; evidence-backed decisions are the product. Corpus expansion is valuable when it improves source coverage, comparability, traceability or analytical usefulness—not as a record-count target.

## 1. The four-layer analytical framework

Every flagship strategy product should test the relationship among the following layers and show the evidence actually available for each. A missing or immaterial layer should be identified honestly rather than filled by inference.

| Layer | Core question | Illustrative evidence | Distinction to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Government policy** | Which interventions or rules change the opportunity, constraint or incentive? | Subsidies, procurement provisions, export and investment restrictions, industrial priorities, binding legal texts | Announcement vs enacted rule vs effective/implemented measure; stated rationale vs legal effect |
| **2. Capital allocation** | Who is committing capital, on what terms, and when does funding become available? | Public financing, loans, equity, grants, offtake, corporate investments, funding conditions, disclosed capital expenditures | Programme ceiling vs award vs agreement vs drawdown/disbursement; public support vs private capital and project cost |
| **3. Industrial execution** | Is the project moving toward the claimed physical or operational outcome? | Permits, construction, commissioning, production, qualification, operational constraints, shipments | Planned capacity vs commissioned capacity vs achieved output; issuer reports vs independent verification |
| **4. Commercial implications** | What do the preceding facts imply for a specific decision-maker? | Cash-flow exposure, procurement cost, security of supply, project returns, capital-allocation options, risk and scenario analysis | Disclosed outcomes vs analyst calculations vs assumption-driven projections |

**Analytical chain:** Policy may alter financing incentives and constraints; financing may enable implementation; implementation may alter a commercial decision. **These are hypotheses to test, not automatic causal claims.** Document the mechanism, timing, countervailing factors and evidence gaps before asserting a connection.

A product must make the chain legible from original-source evidence through calculations to recommendations. A four-layer map is a **method for inquiry**, not a risk score, an inflexible data schema or a requirement to manufacture all four conclusions.

## 2. Applied Strategy product mandate

Build products around a real decision rather than a topic:

1. **Decision-maker:** Name the company, institution or specific stakeholder perspective; for hypothetical users, label the perspective explicitly.
2. **Decision:** State the actionable question, time horizon, alternatives and constraints. Do not imply an actual organization commissioned or endorsed the research.
3. **Evidence and baseline:** Freeze an as-of/source cutoff, record original-source locators and reconcile factual inputs before modeling.
4. **Economic mechanism:** Explain how policy, financing and industrial execution affect cost, revenue, availability, timing, risk or strategic flexibility. Use financial or operational methods suited to that mechanism; do not add a DCF, valuation or quantitative score merely to appear sophisticated.
5. **Options and recommendation:** Compare feasible alternatives under stated criteria; identify conditional recommendations, milestones, triggers and the evidence that would change the conclusion.
6. **Uncertainty:** Separate facts, arithmetic, assumptions and judgment. Show important missing information, competing explanations, and downside cases.

The flagship format is a **consulting-style presentation with a reproducible Excel model when the decision depends materially on quantitative economics**. A short decision memo or deeper technical note may accompany the package. Research dossiers, source reviews and the tracker remain the evidence infrastructure rather than competing product identities.

## 3. Presentation standard

**Default length:** About **10–15 substantive slides**; **20 is the absolute ceiling** for the main deck. Use fewer when the decision can be presented rigorously in fewer. Appendices and backup exhibits must be clearly separated and not used to evade the ceiling.

**Recommended narrative, adjusted to the case:**

- Title, client/stakeholder perspective, scope and research cutoff.
- Executive answer: decision, recommendation, principal quantitative or qualitative support, and decisive caveats.
- Decision context and problem economics; the relevant four-layer chain.
- Evidence-led analysis and alternative scenarios.
- Strategic options compared on consistent, decision-relevant criteria.
- Recommendation, trade-offs, implementation sequence, decision triggers and remaining diligence.
- Evidence/methods notes and links to the model and complete source register.

**Slide craft:**

- Each analytical slide should lead with a **specific, supportable conclusion**, not a generic topic heading; the exhibit must substantiate that claim.
- Use charts only when they reveal relationships; label units, periods, denominators, currencies, assumptions, as-of dates and relevant sources. Keep unlike measures visibly separate.
- Favor charts, decision matrices, waterfall/bridge exhibits, project chronologies, sensitivities and concise explanatory prose over decorative graphics or dense bullet walls.
- Use a restrained, consistent professional sans-serif, readable sizes, whitespace, grid alignment and a consistent visual hierarchy. Follow SMPT's established publication identity where appropriate; avoid imitation of another firm's proprietary branding.
- Preserve analytical connective tissue: explain **why** a result changes the choice, not merely what a chart shows. Avoid exaggerated certainty, promotional tone and formulaic AI-sounding assertions.
- Source notes must be resolvable, preferably to originals. Clearly distinguish sourced values from modeled numbers and hypothetical illustrations.
- The PDF/slide export must be visually inspected slide by slide. Editable source files should remain available when practicable.

The existing [research publication house style](SMPT_RESEARCH_PUBLICATION_HOUSE_STYLE.md) governs its named research formats. This document governs Applied Strategy decks; neither silently overrides factual-source standards.

## 4. Excel model standard

Use an **editable, auditable workbook** when the recommendation relies on financial or operational calculations. Avoid a decorative workbook whose cells reproduce slide numbers without formulas.

A typical workbook separates:

| Area | Minimum expectation |
| --- | --- |
| **Read me / decision** | Purpose, decision-maker, model version, owner, cutoff date, units, scope, limits and navigation |
| **Sources and evidence** | Original-document links/locators, issuer, publication date, as-of date, extracted input, source status and any transformations |
| **Inputs and assumptions** | Distinct observed facts and analyst assumptions; units, dates, currency, rationale, range and editable controls |
| **Calculations** | Transparent cell formulas and traceable intermediate schedules; no unexplained hard-coded output cells |
| **Scenarios and sensitivities** | Base, upside/downside or other decision-relevant cases; change one or multiple drivers as justified; explicit scenario definitions |
| **Decision outputs** | Comparable options, economically meaningful metrics, binding constraints, break-even/threshold conditions and results that reconcile to the deck |
| **Checks** | Integrity and reconciliation tests, range/sign checks, duplicate/overlap controls, missing inputs and independently checked calculations |

**Model discipline:**

- Match model complexity to the question: procurement total-cost comparisons, margin/EBITDA sensitivity, capex and funding-gap analysis, project returns, cash-flow timing or decision trees as justified. Valuation or risk-adjusted NPV is conditional, not mandatory.
- Clearly label **reported**, **derived**, and **modeled** quantities. A sensitivity based on a disclosed company coefficient is not a forecast of future earnings; hypothetical capital-recovery thresholds are not predicted production.
- Preserve underlying tracker amounts in original currencies and existing value roles. Never contaminate SMPT's published aggregation with conversion, speculative modeled amounts or overlapping programme and child commitments. If a standalone case needs a currency-normalized illustration, state the sourced FX rate/date and show the original figures separately; the result is analysis, not a tracker total.
- State and verify whether inputs describe an envelope, signed financing, a drawdown, an actual cash payment, announced capacity, operational output or another distinct status.
- Show uncertainties that matter to the conclusion (e.g. volume, realization timing, unit economics, commodity prices, conversion yields, discount rates, financing conditions, qualification delays). Do not present a convenient assumption as a sourced fact.
- Use actual spreadsheet formulas for calculated values. Check formulas/references, units, sign conventions, scenario switches, and ties between executive slides and workbook. Log any intentional simplifications.
- No model-derived number becomes a source datum unless a new primary source independently establishes that fact and the normal human-gated admission process approves it.

## 5. Evidence and analytical review gate

Before a product is declared **publication-ready**, review the following:

- **Evidence:** Original-source register and cutoff are recorded; material claims and data points are traceable; original language and translation provenance are preserved where applicable. Corporate filings, company announcements and government statements are attributed to their issuers.
- **Four-layer map:** Policy, capital, execution and commercial effects are examined where relevant; unsupported causal links are removed or qualified; material gaps are visible.
- **Financial integrity:** Instruments, roles, currencies, time periods, project stages and ownership/recipient identities have been reconciled. No double counting, misleading aggregation, phantom cash flow or unsupported capacity-to-output conversion.
- **Model QA:** Inputs and assumptions are distinct, formulas recalculate, scenario behavior is tested, sample calculations are independently checked, and quantitative exhibits reconcile exactly with the workbook.
- **Decision value:** The deck names a decision-maker, assesses credible alternatives, states a recommendation with conditions and identifies the next due-diligence question.
- **Editorial QA:** Prose reads as professional analysis, not promotional AI copy; slide titles reflect evidence; exported presentations have been visually inspected; sources and hyperlinks work.
- **Governance:** Human editorial review is completed. Research drafts, private candidates and unpublished findings do not enter the public site, API or data exports through this process.

A missing gate is recorded as a **hold, limitation or open diligence item**, not silently treated as passed. Not every product requires a quantitative model, but any product that uses model outputs must satisfy the relevant workbook checks.

## 6. Release and maintenance boundaries

- This document sets **future research and publication standards**. It does not amend `lib/types.ts`, existing dataset validation, the source hierarchy, or the legal/financial status definitions in `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `README.md` and the public methodology.
- Separate **source-data release**, **analytical product release** and **format-only revision**. Do not silently update a frozen research cutoff or a published analysis when restyling a deck.
- Research products and their evidence/model files should carry stable IDs, dates, version notes and references to the exact underlying inputs. Store live status in product-specific documentation or Git history rather than embedding volatile counts in this standard.
- Maintain method flexibility. The four-layer approach is the durable analytical lens; slide structures and model techniques must be adapted to the real decision.
- External feedback and actual research use are preferred indicators of usefulness; record count, slide count and production volume are not success metrics.
