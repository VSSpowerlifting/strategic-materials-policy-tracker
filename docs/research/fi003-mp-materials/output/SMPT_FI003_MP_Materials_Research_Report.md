# MP Materials’ 10X Financing: Cash, Covenants, and Construction Risk

**SMPT Financial Intelligence 003 · Manuscript for Benjamin and Jiho**

**Evidence through 6 November 2026 · Publication HOLD**

MP Materials, a U.S. rare-earth miner and processor, agreed in July 2025 to build 10X, its second domestic factory for rare-earth permanent magnets. The project needed money for construction, equipment and commissioning, the process of testing and bringing the plant into service. The U.S. Department of Defense agreed to purchase eligible magnet output under an offtake agreement, but the plant had to reach commercial operation before its operating payments could begin. The transaction agreement put development through start-up at an estimated $1.25 billion. MP needed a way to pay for that work while 10X was still unable to generate operating cash. [July 2025 Form 8-K, Item 1.01][s01] · [Transaction agreement, recitals and §2.05][s02] · [Offtake agreement, §§1.2, 3.1 and 6.1–6.4][s03]

MP secured a joint construction commitment, then raised **$724.2 million in net common equity proceeds** and put a **$275 million corporate revolver** in place. A revolver is a credit line that allows borrowing and repayment within a commitment limit. The original construction commitment expired undrawn. The offering reduced a nonparticipating holder’s ownership percentage by **7.68% on an isolated basis**. In exchange, MP obtained cash it could deploy for permitted uses without meeting another loan draw condition or scheduling principal and interest payments on that capital. The financing decision was defensible because the company was selling shares while it could and still faced years of spending before 10X could collect operating receipts. The record establishes that trade-off. Management’s private reasons and the relative cost of the original loan remain unknown. [2025 Form 10-K, Notes 3, 10 and 18][s11] · [Prospectus supplement, pp. S-7 and S-13][s06] · [Model 1.0, financing economics][economics]

For a CFO planning a comparable build, fund the construction base case with equity if the issue price is acceptable and permitted cash can carry the project through a stressed start-up. Keep a committed revolver for letters of credit and overruns that the facility can still fund in the downside case. MP relied on its balance sheet during the build and maintained a corporate credit line. At 30 September 2026, the company reported $1.338 billion of cash and short-term investments and $203 million of remaining borrowing capacity. Those company-wide figures give a starting point for a funding test. The answer depends on when the remaining bills fall due, when cash comes in and whether the line is drawable at the low point. [September 2026 Form 10-Q, balance sheet, Note 8 and MD&A][s19] · [Draft 1.1 recommendation and decision conditions][deck]

## 1. What shareholders bought with dilution

The transaction agreement required MP to deliver an executed financing commitment on 9 July 2025 and a binding commitment by the 11 July closing. JPMorgan Chase Funding Inc. and Goldman Sachs Bank USA, with their affiliates, committed secured financing of at least $1 billion in aggregate. The agreement called for at least $650 million to be allocated specifically to 10X construction and up to $600 million of MP’s existing cash across 10X, facility improvements and the samarium project. It allowed dollar-for-dollar reductions for certain equity or debt raises and certain unrestricted cash. MP later disclosed $7.4 million in nonrefundable commitment and structuring fees. Those fees paid for a conditional financing route that MP could present at closing and replace if other capital became available. The filings give the aggregate fee and leave individual bank economics undisclosed. [Transaction agreement, §§2.05–2.07][s02] · [July 2025 Form 8-K, Item 1.01][s01] · [2025 Form 10-K, Note 3][s11]

The government agreement also required MP to seek at least $350 million of incremental net financing by the 45th day after closing. If MP fell short under the agreement, Defense was to buy additional preferred stock for the shortfall, subject to a notice and a separate closing. The July offering exceeded the threshold. That fallback was distinct from the initial $400 million preferred investment. It also came with a warrant already issued at closing, in consideration for the fallback and other undertakings. Avoiding an additional preferred purchase left that warrant in place. Unless Defense consented, the use-of-funds covenant reserved specified funding sources, including incremental proceeds up to the $350 million threshold, for the designated projects. It separately restricted 10X facility funding to 10X through completion and commissioning. The public prospectus described broader uses for the offering, including expansion and general corporate purposes. The contract and the prospectus together leave the exact allocation of offering proceeds to 10X undisclosed. [Transaction agreement, recitals and §§2.03, 6.01(a)–(g)][s02] · [Prospectus supplement, Use of Proceeds][s06] · [2025 Form 10-K, Notes 3 and 18][s11]

MP priced the offering on 16 July 2025 at $55 a share and issued 13,590,908 shares, including the full exercise of the underwriters’ option. Underwriting discounts and other expenses reduced approximately $747.5 million of gross proceeds to the reported $724.2 million net. MP signed the $275 million revolver on 25 August and reported that the offering and revolver reduced the original commitment dollar for dollar until it expired undrawn on 26 August. Adding final net equity and the revolver limit gives $999.2 million. The calculation combines cash with capacity and is $0.8 million below the $1 billion reference. Using proceeds after the underwriting discount and before other offering expenses gives a combined figure slightly above $1 billion. The filed record establishes the undrawn expiration and leaves the exact cancellation calculation open. [Underwriting agreement, Schedule B][s07] · [Credit agreement, cover and §1.01][s09] · [2025 Form 10-K, Notes 3 and 18][s11] · [Model 1.0, replacement schedule][replacement]

MP was the issuer, the revolver’s parent borrower and the sponsor responsible for delivering the project. J.P. Morgan says it was the sole financial adviser and led the arrangement of the construction commitment. The filings identify JPMorgan and Goldman as joint providers of that commitment. In the stock offering, J.P. Morgan Securities and Goldman Sachs & Co. were joint lead bookrunners, the firms managing the sale. Each agreed to purchase 4,727,272 initial shares, approximately 40% apiece. Morgan Stanley was a bookrunner and agreed to purchase 2,363,637 initial shares, approximately 20%. These quantities describe underwriting obligations for the initial tranche. The agreements leave retained positions and bank-by-bank fees undisclosed. [J.P. Morgan’s transaction account][s15] · [Prospectus supplement, cover and Underwriting][s06] · [Underwriting agreement, Schedule A][s07] · [2025 Form 10-K, Note 3][s11]

For the corporate facility, JPMorgan Chase Bank became administrative and collateral agent, responsible for administering the facility and acting for lenders with respect to pledged assets. It also signed as a lender and letter-of-credit issuer. Goldman Sachs Bank USA and Morgan Stanley Senior Funding joined JPMorgan as lead arrangers and bookrunners. Citibank and BofA Securities were co-syndication agents. Citibank and Bank of America, N.A. signed alongside the three arrangers as lenders and letter-of-credit issuers. JPMorgan’s role spans all three transactions, with distinct responsibilities shared across the banks. [Credit agreement, cover and signature pages][s09]

**The ownership calculation.** The prospectus gives 163,477,816 basic shares outstanding on 9 July. Adding only the 13,590,908 offering shares gives an isolated base of 177,068,724. The new shares divided by that enlarged base yield 7.68%, leaving prior shares at 92.32% of the total. Dividing the same issuance by the original base gives the 8.31% increase in share count. The calculation holds prior shareholdings fixed and excludes other issuances, government preferred conversion, warrants, convertible debt and options. It measures the change in ownership percentage attributable to the offering. The dollar value of what shareholders gave up depends on the future value of their residual claim after other obligations are paid. [Prospectus supplement, The Offering, p. S-7][s06] · [2025 Form 10-K, Note 18][s11] · [Model 1.0, Financing!B18:B24][economics]

## 2. When construction debt would have been preferable

Debt could have preserved existing holders’ ownership while providing a dedicated source of construction money. The strongest version of that case is a facility that lends against a credible cost schedule, lets MP draw as bills fall due, and defers principal until the project is producing and collecting enough cash to pay it. A long maturity, room for delays in the draw conditions and limits on the lender’s ability to pursue the parent would make the comparison more favorable to debt. If the plant’s cash after operating costs, taxes, working capital and sustaining capital could service that loan even in a delayed ramp, shareholders could retain more of the upside. Debt would also have been worth retaining if the equity market had closed or the share price required materially more dilution for the same cash. Section 2.05 permits financing raised at the project company without recourse to MP, meaning lenders would generally look to the project for repayment. The filed materials leave open whether the joint commitment offered that protection. [Transaction agreement, §2.05][s02] · [Draft 1.1, financing options and decision conditions][deck]

The decision turns on the original loan’s interest rate, fees, maturity, principal repayment schedule, draw and completion conditions, security and parent guarantees. MP disclosed commitment and structuring fees. The complete operative commitment letter and final terms in those categories were unavailable in the reviewed filings. The other side of the comparison needs dated construction and ramp costs, shipment and collection dates, qualifying contract volumes and costs, and the working capital needed to get through ramp-up. The offtake supports sales of qualifying output and provides a capacity-adjusted earnings threshold from commercial operation until the contracted production milestone. Its payment schedule and eligibility rules matter more to debt service than the headline threshold. A low rate offers little protection if the first principal payment comes before reliable collections or a delay blocks the next draw. [Transaction agreement, §2.05][s02] · [Offtake agreement, §§1.2, 3.1 and 6.1–6.4, 6.8][s03] · [2025 Form 10-K, Note 3][s11]

**What MP could know in July 2025.** MP had a conditional debt commitment, an agreed construction and start-up estimate, and government support tied to specific products and uses. It knew commercial operation would precede 10X operating payments. The agreement called for a financing raise but left the choice of instrument to MP. An executable offering let management satisfy that requirement, put cash on the balance sheet and avoid debt service on the amount raised. Apple’s 15 July agreement for magnets from MP’s existing Independence facility and recycling work at Mountain Pass preceded the 16 July pricing. That timing makes an ordinary equity takeout during an open market window the strongest explanation in the public record. It leaves open how much demand or pricing reflected Apple, Defense support or other factors. The August revolver and later collections belong to the assessment of how the choice played out. [Transaction agreement, recitals and §§2.05, 6.01][s02] · [Offtake agreement, §§1.2, 6.1–6.2][s03] · [Apple agreement, Form 8-K, Item 8.01][s17] · [Prospectus supplement, cover and Use of Proceeds][s06]

**What happened afterward.** In its 6 November 2026 filing, MP said construction was underway in Northlake, Texas. It expected to invest more than $1.25 billion and begin commissioning in 2028. Company cash and short-term investments fell by $492.375 million between year-end 2025 and 30 September 2026. Across the business, MP spent $420.250 million in cash on property, plant and equipment and had $91.145 million of net financing outflows. Operations used $7.757 million in cash even with $110.9 million of Defense price-protection receipts. Those receipts offset eligible shortfalls on neodymium-praseodymium, a rare-earth input to magnets. MP reported compliance with the credit agreement and zero revolver borrowings at quarter-end. The record shows the balance sheet absorbing the build period while the credit line supported letters of credit. The spending and cash balances cover the whole company, so they cannot establish a 10X funding gap or rank the original debt against equity. [September 2026 Form 10-Q, cash-flow statement, Notes 4 and 8, and MD&A][s19] · [Price protection agreement, §§2–3][s04] · [Model 1.0, liquidity bridge][liquidity]

## 3. How much room MP had if commissioning slipped

A letter of credit is a bank’s promise to pay a beneficiary if the letter’s conditions are met. It uses part of the revolver without putting loan proceeds in MP’s account. The liquidity figures below describe different dates and obligations. Adding them would overstate the cash available to finish the plant.

**The first loss is time.** Under offtake §1.2, the commercial operation date requires the project company to have commenced operations capable of producing magnets and to have given Defense notice. Section 6.2 schedules the first facilitation payment for the first day of the following quarter, with a partial-quarter adjustment where applicable. Until the production milestone, the annual threshold is scaled by estimated capacity relative to the contractual target. Limited reimbursements can precede commercial operation. Section 6.4.1 excludes initially expected construction and start-up costs from its incremental-cost provision. If a commissioning slip pushes commercial operation into a later quarter, the first facilitation payment moves with it. MP must cover ongoing spending for longer while waiting for those receipts. [Offtake agreement, §§1.2, 6.1–6.4][s03]

**The next loss can be access to credit.** Before the covenant trigger, §7.11(a) requires at least $500 million of unrestricted cash and qualifying cash equivalents at the end of each test period, which ends at a fiscal quarter-end. If delayed receipts and continuing spend take MP below that amount at a pre-trigger test date, the breach is an Event of Default under §8.01(b). Section 4.02(b) conditions subsequent credit extensions on the absence of a Default, including one caused by the extension or use of its proceeds. Section 1.01 applies this to borrowings and to issuance, extension, renewal or increase of letters of credit. Section 8.02 allows the agent to terminate commitments, accelerate outstanding loans and require cash collateral for outstanding letters of credit during a continuing Event of Default. The agent must act at the required lenders’ request. These are scenario consequences. MP reported compliance at 30 September 2026. [Credit agreement, §§1.01, 4.02, 7.11(a), 8.01(b) and 8.02][s09] · [September 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8][s19]

A separate risk arises if Defense payments are actually suspended or released under the agreements named in the credit contract. The offtake’s §8.4.1(a), for example, allows Defense to suspend its performance after a project-company payment default remains uncured for 45 days after written notice, or longer if §8.3 provides a longer period. The underlying §8.3.1 default requires an undisputed amount owed to Defense to remain unpaid for 60 days after it is due. On the report’s reading of credit agreement §§1.01, 4.02(b) and 8.01(m)(i)(2), an actual suspension or release that meets that clause can block new credit immediately as a Default. A continuing condition becomes an Event of Default after 60 days, extended by 30 days if MP delivers the required officer certificate on day 60 with qualifying unrestricted cash and equivalents of at least 125% of commitments. That is $343.75 million at the $275 million commitment level. The extension delays remedies for that continuing condition while the Default continues to block new credit. A late receipt, an appropriation shortfall or a missed commissioning date establishes none of those triggers by itself. The earlier point at which a right to suspend exists, but has yet to be exercised, remains open to interpretation. The timing and application of these provisions warrant counsel review. [Offtake agreement, §§8.3.1 and 8.4.1(a)][s03] · [Credit agreement, §§1.01, 4.02, 8.01(m) and 8.02][s09] · [Draft 1.1, default and cash-test arithmetic][deck]

A separate provision, §8.01(m)(ii), reaches a default after applicable cure periods if it gives the counterparty a right to terminate the relevant Defense agreement. Under transaction agreement §9.02(a)(ii), Defense can terminate the offtake or price protection agreement, or both, for a qualifying uncured material breach of MP’s 10X development, construction or commissioning and start-up obligations. The provision specifies a cure period of at least 120 days for a qualifying breach other than failure to pay and at least 90 days’ prior written notice to declare a company default. The credit provision turns on the termination right. Credit extensions also require specified representations to hold when made, including the representation concerning a material adverse effect. A CFO should reassess draw eligibility when a delay puts contractual performance or those representations in question. [Transaction agreement, §9.02(a)(ii)][s02] · [Credit agreement, §§4.02(a), 5.05(b) and 8.01(m)(ii)][s09]

MP must also plan for a change in the financial tests before its expected commissioning year. The trigger is the earlier delivery of a compliance certificate showing at least $400 million of covenant EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, as adjusted under the credit contract), or the required financial statements for the quarter ending 30 June 2027. From the first fiscal quarter-end after that delivery, net debt as defined in the agreement may be at most 4.00 times covenant EBITDA, subject to the specified acquisition adjustments. Covenant EBITDA must cover contractual cash interest expense at least 3.00 times. On ordinary filing timing those tests would begin by the September 2027 quarter-end. The agreement allows qualifying Defense amounts to enter covenant EBITDA subject to a compliance-certificate calculation and a reversal for later duplicative cash. That adjustment can support a ratio before the related cash is collected. MP’s actual compliance certificates and covenant EBITDA are unavailable in the reviewed disclosures. [Credit agreement, §§1.01, 6.01(b), 6.02(a) and 7.11][s09] · [September 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8 and MD&A][s19]

The deck’s screening calculation puts the leverage break-even at about $232 million of covenant EBITDA under the 50% cash-netting reading, or about $209 million under the 100% reading. It uses $1,043.133 million of September principal as a proxy for covenant debt, assumes enough eligible cash to reach the cap, and assumes trigger-date EBITDA is no higher than test-period EBITDA. At four times leverage, the calculation divides principal by 4.5 or 5.0, respectively. The contract describes both cash deductions in its definition of net indebtedness. The report uses $232 million as a conservative screen pending interpretation of which cap applies. Actual debt, eligible cash, covenant earnings and any draw would change the result. [Credit agreement, §1.01 and §7.11(b)][s09] · [September 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8][s19] · [Draft 1.1, covenant timing and decision conditions][deck]

A defensible completion-funding estimate requires a dated 10X cost-to-complete schedule, including unpaid commitments, equipment payments, commissioning contingencies and ramp working capital. Each period must be matched to cash that MP can use after other corporate needs, and to collectible reimbursements and operating payments under a delayed case. The transaction agreement requires MP to report progress and estimated completion cost to Defense and to use reasonable best efforts to raise more capital if the specified sources are or are projected to be insufficient. It gives Defense access to information on uses and remaining funding and provides an efforts-based standard for construction timing. The public filings supply the investment estimate and company-wide spending. They leave the remaining project schedule and its cash trough unknown. A later or deeper trough could call for new equity, separately drawable debt, a covenant amendment or a change to spending before the shortfall arrives. An equity-only plan can make sense if permitted cash covers the downside and MP can meet its letter-of-credit needs separately. MP’s revolver already supported $72 million of those instruments at quarter-end, so that alternative needs a credible substitute and a price for it. [Transaction agreement, §§6.04(c)–(d), 6.13 and 7.01(e)][s02] · [September 2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A][s19]

## CFO judgment and question for Jiho

Take the equity while the price is acceptable and preserve committed credit for uses it can actually fund. Before releasing a construction commitment, test settled cash and permitted uses through the delayed start-up case. If the plan needs the revolver in a period when a draw condition could fail, secure cash or a facility with different conditions before that period. If construction debt can preserve ownership while financing that same downside through the first reliable collections, it deserves priority over further dilution. The evidence supports MP’s equity-first choice as defensible. A fully funded plan or a least-cost capital structure remains unproven.

**Question for Jiho.** In a downside case that moves the first 10X operating receipts into a later quarter, would the cost-to-complete and collection schedule leave MP with enough permitted cash to reach those receipts and satisfy the intervening covenant tests if new revolver draws were unavailable? That answer would determine whether the revolver provides useful secondary capacity or whether the financing plan depends on arranging independent funds before commissioning.
