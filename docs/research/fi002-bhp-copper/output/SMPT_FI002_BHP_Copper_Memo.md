# SMPT FI002 Executive Brief
Cutoff: 5 November 2026. Independent SMPT research. No affiliation, sponsorship or endorsement by Goldman Sachs or BHP.

## Goldman Sachs’ Copper Outlook and BHP’s $6 Billion Escondida Decision
### Judgment
Maintain funded engineering, permitting and conditional FID readiness for the Escondida New Concentrator. Selectively accelerate preparation that resolves uncertainty or protects a credible replacement timetable. Public disclosures cannot establish whether construction acceleration or deferral has the highest expected value.

### Why Goldman matters
The reported October forecast raised Goldman’s calendar-2027 average copper price to $15,400/t from $13,800/t. BHP’s disclosed FY2026 sensitivity of $39m of underlying EBITDA per US cent/lb translates the $1,600/t revision into $2.83bn of hypothetical static EBITDA exposure. The $2,400/t gap to the World Bank’s $13,000/t forecast translates into $4.25bn on the same basis. Neither result is a 2027 earnings forecast or a project funding allowance.

### The investment is about replacement
The $5.4–6.3bn nominal gross plant budget replaces Los Colorados. The 230–270ktpa facility range cannot be treated as wholly incremental mine output. BHP guides to a 16–18% IRR at a long-term $4.76/lb copper assumption, approximately $10,494/t. First production targets 2031–32. A bullish 2027 price can improve financing flexibility without proving lifetime investment returns.

### What the original analysis contributes
The research compares price expectations at a common disclosed earnings-exposure scale and tests the benefits needed to recover a replacement budget. It finds that a seemingly precise production hurdle is highly sensitive to benefit duration, margin and the unavailable no-build mine plan. That limits the case for a blanket recommendation to accelerate or stage construction.

## Decision conditions and evidence that would change the judgment
### How to interpret 199ktpa
Five equal $1.17bn capital draws in 2027–31, a 10% nominal discount rate and 20 full benefit years from 2032 imply $839m of annual after-tax benefit to recover $5.85bn. At an assumed $10,494/t price, $4,000/t all-in net burden and 35% cash-tax wedge, that equals 198.8ktpa of preserved or added benefit. The burden is not C1 cost. The screen estimates neither actual project NPV nor BHP’s IRR.

### What changes the screen
Crediting only ten benefit years from 2034 raises the required equivalent to 333ktpa. Combined $8,500/t copper, 20% more capital and two years of benefit delay produces 416ktpa. BHP’s output averaging window is FY2034–43, not a disclosed twenty-year flat schedule. The results demonstrate assumption risk, not project failure or success.

### Funding discipline
FY2026 operating cash of $21.778bn less net investing outflow of $12.011bn gives $9.767bn of FCF. After $6.756bn of BHP cash dividends and $2.355bn of NCI dividends, $0.656bn remains before other financing. This is history, not a forecast. BHP’s cumulative attributable Copper FCF scenarios of $12bn in FY2027–31 and $24bn in FY2032–35 already include modeled investment. Do not deduct concentrator capital again.

### Evidence for a different decision
Accelerate construction if a permitted, executable plan shows avoided continuity losses worth more than the premium and added risk. Stage or re-scope if returns weaken and alternative processing can preserve production cheaply. The next most valuable inputs are annual with-build and no-build output, alternate circuits, sustaining cost, closure, tax and capital schedules. Near-term bank price targets cannot resolve those unknowns.
