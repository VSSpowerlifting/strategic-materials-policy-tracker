# SMPT Editorial Authenticity & Visual Integrity Standard

**Version:** 1.0 · **Adopted:** 5 November 2026  
**Applies to:** New and revised SMPT research presentations and their analytical copy, figures, charts, diagrams, editable assets and PDF exports.  
**Companion documents:** [Presentation Standard v2](SMPT_PRESENTATION_STANDARD_V2.md) · [Exhibit and Chart Rules](EXHIBIT_AND_CHART_RULES.md) · [Four-Layer Analytical Framework](../../research/SMPT_APPLIED_STRATEGY_STANDARD.md)

## Mandate

Publish a defensible argument in clear, natural language and purposeful, accurate visuals. Every major statement should convey evidence, an economic mechanism, an implication, or a material limitation. Every exhibit should make an important comparison or relationship easier to understand. The final file should remain clear to an independent reader.

**This is a publication-quality standard, not an AI detector or a method for concealing AI use.** Stylistic signals are editorial prompts; none proves a text or image was AI-generated. Keep authorship, sourcing, contributor and AI-assistance disclosures accurate under the applicable publication policy. Agents may assist with drafting, editing, analysis, chart construction and QA, subject to the same evidence and human-approval gates as other work.

## 1. Affirmative writing: the default

### State the substantive finding directly

A common low-quality drafting pattern creates drama by dismissing a simplistic interpretation before presenting the real point. Prefer to **lead with the financial, strategic or policy proposition** and then explain its mechanism.

Treat these as *review triggers*:

- "not just X, but Y" / "not merely X, but Y" / "isn't simply"
- "more than just X" / "goes beyond X" / "extends beyond X"
- "the question is not whether X, but how Y"
- "rather than merely X, Y"
- "X is no longer enough; Y is essential"
- "this is not about X; it is about Y"
- Repeated "while X, Y" contrasts without a meaningful analytical comparison

**Before:** "The government's investment is not merely a financing package; it is a catalyst for long-term industrial resilience."

**After:** "The investment supplies equity and repayable debt for Hemerdon's restart. The loan introduces an obligation that will need to be met or refinanced as the company advances toward commercial production."

**Before:** "The issue isn't just the cost increase, but how it impacts the broader financial landscape."

**After:** "Higher project expenditure can increase financing requirements when it precedes commercial receipts. The magnitude depends on the remaining capital budget, available funding and timing of collections."

**Before:** "FI001 is more than a funding story; it is a strategic assessment."

**After:** "FI001 reconstructs the financing instruments and tests how expenditure, receipt timing and loan obligations affect the restart's financial execution."

These rewrites strengthen the **analysis**, rather than exchanging one stock phrase for another.

### Preserve factual negation and meaningful contrast

Rhetorical negation and technical negation serve different purposes.

- An **uncommitted £10m accordion is not cash received**: a necessary instrument-status distinction.
- An **illustrative scenario does not establish a current funding deficit**: a necessary inference boundary.
- A reported offtake agreement **does not establish completed delivery or cash collection**: a necessary operational-evidence distinction.

Keep statements like these when omission could mislead. Move nonessential disclaimers to methodology and retain the precise one at the point of risk. Contrast is useful when the competing interpretations genuinely matter.

**Editorial rule:** Flag manufactured negation for review; **never automatically delete "not", "no", "without", or "uncommitted"**. A mechanical ban would damage financial accuracy.

## 2. Natural analytical reasoning

A strong paragraph develops a thought. The writer establishes the observation, explains the contractual/economic mechanism, connects it to the project, and follows through to a decision implication or material uncertainty. These are **questions to answer**, not a fixed paragraph formula.

- Vary sentence lengths according to analytical need. Use short sentences for decisive findings and longer sentences to connect variables or qualify causal inferences.
- Prefer concrete subjects and verbs: "The £25m loan requires repayment" over "A critical financial obligation is underscored."
- Explain *why* a cost, timing, contractual or policy change matters. A sentence that merely restates the chart label offers little analytical value.
- Sustain terminology across memo, slides, appendix and model. Distinguish announced, authorized, selected, executed, drawn, spent, produced, delivered and collected.
- Use connective reasoning where valuable: cost increase → financing need → maturity exposure → monitoring question. Preserve contingent terms and real dates.
- Cut formulaic rhetoric: "navigating the complex landscape", "critical juncture", "unlocking potential", "at the intersection", "serves as a testament", "underscores the importance", and repeated "the path forward".
- Review repeated triads, decorative em dashes, uniform paragraph lengths, generic slide conclusions and invented urgency. These are **patterns to inspect**, not token-count violations.
- A slide uses a concise assertion title plus exhibits and an interpretive note; a memo uses fuller paragraphs. Neither should become a paste of the other.

### Specificity challenge

Could the statement appear unchanged in a report on a completely different company? If so, add the relevant instrument, event, source, timing, amount, mechanism or decision—or remove it.

**Generic:** "Commercial execution is essential for long-term resilience."

**Specific:** "The core loan's short tenor overlaps with Hemerdon's commissioning and ramp-up period, making the timing of saleable output, customer receipts and refinancing arrangements material to creditor exposure."

Make the source of each conclusion explicit: **documented fact**, **management/government claim**, **SMPT calculation**, **assumption**, or **analyst inference**.

## 3. Visual reasoning: evidence before ornament

Choose a layout after defining the comparison the reader needs. Test whether each shape, arrow, color or chart changes what the reader can understand.

### Patterns requiring redesign or justification

| Pattern | Failure mode | Better option |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated three-card grids with generic icons | Uniform shape substitutes for analytical structure | A ranked comparison or an exhibit with genuinely different statuses |
| Arrows between generic terms ("policy → investment → resilience") | Implies causality without evidence | Dated dependency timeline separating observed events and pending outcomes |
| Circular/flow diagrams with no measurable relationships | Apparent complexity without new information | An instrument matrix or a directly labeled process with actual dependencies |
| KPI wallpaper, gauges, decorative maps or stock illustration | Impressive surface but no decision value | One carefully selected, source-verified comparison |
| Smooth synthetic curves, dramatic 3D bars or pie overload | Imprecise or misleading quantitative communication | Direct-value bars, honest time series, scenario matrices, or relevant tables |
| Almost identical layouts on every slide | Mechanical, repetitive reading experience | Layout variation driven by the analytical question |

**A three-column slide is legitimate when three comparable categories genuinely exist.** Visual symmetry is a design choice, never evidence that the categories are equivalent.

Apply the [visual-repertoire workflow](VISUAL_REPERTOIRE_AND_REFERENCES.md): plan the exhibit family from the relationship, review the contact sheet for repetitive composition, and document any intentional repetition under gates C/E. Variety should improve attention and comprehension while preserving a consistent SMPT identity. Chart-type counts alone cannot pass this review.

### Graphics and AI generation

- Construct **quantitative charts from verified numeric data**: controlling workbook, source-linked dataset, or reproducible script. Keep edits and labels consistent with source values.
- Image-generation output may support **clearly labeled conceptual artwork**, subject to rights and disclosure requirements. It cannot supply authoritative data points, factual facility imagery, source-document screenshots, geographic boundaries, financial charts or verification.
- Preserve editability: native PowerPoint text/shapes/charts where practical; record source input and generator paths for computed figures.
- Verify complete financial classification. Use different line/outline/status treatment for uncommitted versus committed, management targets versus realized results, and reported versus independently verified activity.
- A timeline arrow communicates **sequence** unless a documented basis supports causality. Show the uncertainty directly.
- Original layouts must follow the SMPT graphite/cream/gold palette, Archivo typography, direct labels, readable sourcing and the hard **20-slide total cap**.

### Accessibility

Review actual exported files for contrast, reading order, legibility, meaningful hyperlinks and alt text on informative images/charts. Color alone cannot carry an instrument or evidence status. Embedded text in a bitmap should never be the only way to access critical information.

## 4. Production contract for agents and engineers

**Input contract** — Before drafting, locate:
1. Governing analytical question and audience.
2. Accepted evidence/source register and dates.
3. Controlling model, reproducible inputs and validation result.
4. Relevant publication status and source rights.
5. This standard, Presentation Standard v2 and the exhibit rules.

**Slide-spec contract** — For each intended analytical slide, record:
- Working assertion title; exact claim and evidentiary class.
- The comparison/relationship the exhibit must expose.
- Chart or layout type, data and source paths, units and dated basis.
- One concise explanation of the financial or strategic mechanism.
- Material caveat, accessibility requirements and whether a source figure needs permission.

The spec can be a small table/Markdown note. Avoid generating decorative illustrations first and inventing analysis to justify them afterward.

**Build contract** — Produce editable PPTX, matching PDF and traceable chart data. Preserve the accepted financial model and original research when doing editorial/design work. Keep any publication successor versioned.

**Review contract** — Run the five gates below. Use automated checks for objective properties and human/agent review for semantic/editorial judgment.

**Stop contract** — Return PASS / REVISE / HOLD findings and the exact artifacts reviewed. Agent completion is **READY FOR HUMAN PUBLICATION REVIEW**, never self-authorized public release.

## 5. Five independent review gates

| Gate | Reviewer task | Typical observable evidence | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Analytical substance** | Challenge whether the story contains original, supported reasoning, mechanisms and decision implications | Headline sequence, claim-to-evidence trace, specific economic logic | PASS / REVISE / HOLD |
| **B. Prose authenticity** | Read full copy; flag rhetorical negation, stock language, repeated cadence, shallow conclusions and poor connective tissue | Before/after sample edits; revised source text | PASS / REVISE / HOLD |
| **C. Visual integrity** | Render **every slide**; inspect contact sheet and full-size slides for generic decoration, misleading visuals, clipping, font substitution and accessibility | Rendered-slide inventory, screenshots, corrected defects | PASS / REVISE / HOLD |
| **D. Quantitative/source fidelity** | Independently check material figures, status, units, source links, chronology and underlying model outputs | Reconciliation table, checked references, test results | PASS / REVISE / HOLD |
| **E. Publication coherence** | Read title-only storyline and whole deck; test reader comprehension, duplicated slides, authorial consistency and relevance | Editorial summary and outstanding issues | PASS / REVISE / HOLD |

### Objective checks versus editorial checks

**Good candidates for deterministic automation:** slide count (all slides ≤20), required file presence, numeric/model reconciliation, cited URLs, chart-value extraction where supported, exact duplicate headlines, missing metadata, font use in editable files, detected overflow and broken source links. Some require rendering, extraction or execution in the relevant Office environment. Record what actually ran.

**Heuristic flags for editor review:** contrastive-negation patterns, boilerplate phrases, unusually repetitive three-card layouts, patterned sentence openings, excessive same-length bullets. **Flags do not prove AI authorship, and zero flags do not prove editorial quality.** Never mass-rewrite or censor technical negation.

**Human/editorial judgment:** whether the financial argument is intellectually sound, a chart supports the precise claim, the prose has coherent thought, a diagram implies false causality, an illustration has a legitimate purpose, and a rights restriction permits reproduction.

### Hard holds

Any of these blocks publication until resolved:
- Fabricated sources, images presented as factual evidence, or material unsupported claims.
- Incorrect material figures, mislabeled units or irreconcilable exhibits.
- Misclassified financing status or scenarios presented as observed financial outcomes.
- Materially misleading chart geometry or implied causality.
- Copyright/source-rights violations, missing material assumptions, or unapproved use of restricted reference screenshots.
- Missing human publication authorization.

A valid financial caveat, cautious qualifier or justified visual pattern **never** triggers HOLD merely because it resembles an alleged AI-writing tell.

Copy into the publication's quality-control report; link to exact files, versions and sources.

Only mark a gate PASS when its stated evidence was examined. Use REVISE for addressable quality issues; use HOLD for material accuracy, provenance, rights or authorization issues. This document sets an evaluation standard; it does not claim that an independent professional has performed or certified any review.

## 7. Mandatory handoff for AI-assisted deck creation

> Read the accepted research record, SMPT Presentation Standard v2, the Exhibit and Chart Rules and this authenticity standard. Draft a claim-by-claim storyline before rendering slides. Prefer direct, affirmative explanations; review "not merely X but Y" and similar contrasts and preserve necessary factual negations. Develop connective financial reasoning. Construct editable quantitative exhibits from the controlling data; choose graphic forms for analytical utility and visibly distinguish evidence statuses. Inspect every rendered slide, reconcile its figures and citations, and record the five gate outcomes in the QC report. Stop at READY FOR HUMAN PUBLICATION REVIEW, with no merge, deployment, or automatic republication.

## 8. Adoption and boundaries

Apply to newly authored and revised SMPT research decks, including the **planned FI001 v2 redesign**. The current live FI001 files, original models and approved factual statements remain intact until a separately approved, versioned replacement passes publication review. The existing 20-slide cap and colors remain unchanged; the presentation typography is now Archivo under Presentation Standard v2.1.
