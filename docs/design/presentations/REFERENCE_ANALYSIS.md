# Reference analysis: consulting and analytical research decks

5 November 2026 · Companion to [SMPT Presentation Standard v2](SMPT_PRESENTATION_STANDARD_V2.md)

**6 November extension:** [Visual Repertoire & Consulting References](VISUAL_REPERTOIRE_AND_REFERENCES.md) adds verified publisher links and exact page/figure locators, original SMPT examples and a production workflow for varied exhibits. This older comparison remains historical; Archivo under the current presentation standard controls typography.

This is **design research**, not permission to reproduce branded consulting assets. Four PDF examples were supplied for internal study. Original decks are *not* redistributed here; the linked institutional publications and the original SMPT example SVGs provide public reference points.

## A. Four decks supplied for comparison

### McKinsey & Company — Global Economics Intelligence, Global Summary (April 2023; 28 PDF pages)

- **Page 3:** comparative country GDP-forecast bars alongside explanatory economic commentary. Useful pattern: show measure, units, dates and source at bottom, with the interpretation close to the chart.
- **Pages 7, 10, 13:** quantitative time-series and forecast comparisons with consistent axis and source conventions.
- **Pages 8, 11, 14:** repeatable indicator heatmaps show how a monitoring/reporting deck can make heterogeneous observations comparable.
- **Strength to adapt:** analytical density and traceable comparisons.
- **Avoid:** turning a short SMPT executive deck into a newspaper-like text report or relying on small lettering everywhere.

The supplied PDF's embedded typefaces include **Georgia Bold (large headings) and Arial (body)**. That is document-specific, not a claim about McKinsey's protected house fonts.

### McKinsey & Company — Investment and Industrial Policy: A Perspective on the Future (UNCTAD, 1 October 2018; 23 PDF pages)

- **Pages 3–4:** numerical trends across multiple time periods.
- **Page 11:** a comparative contributions exhibit makes the economic story legible without a long paragraph.
- **Pages 18–19:** split comparisons and compact small multiples.
- **Strength to adapt:** choose exhibit structures that clarify the denominator, time period and economics of change.
- **Avoid:** copying dated infographic ornaments or treating simultaneous data series as causal mechanisms.

### BCG and Semiconductor Industry Association — The Growing Challenge of Semiconductor Design Leadership (December 2022; 16 PDF pages)

- **Page 5:** revenue-share chart with an aligned set of business-relevant comparators; shows how the dominant data can occupy most of the slide.
- **Page 7:** multi-decade industry leaders table conveys the turnover pattern efficiently.
- **Page 9:** escalating design-cost bar chart pairs a declarative title with directly labeled figures.
- **Pages 11–13:** workforce arithmetic, regional demand and market-share forecast exhibits create a sequential argument.
- **Strength to adapt:** one principal exhibit per slide, disciplined numerical hierarchy and clear statement titles.
- **Avoid:** assuming its green palette, trademark treatment or exact layouts are suitable for SMPT.

### BCG — New York COVID-19 Preliminary Economic Impact Assessment (April 2020; 38 PDF pages)

- **Pages 13–14:** multiple industry measures and a comparable row-based exhibit.
- **Page 17:** modeled GDP loss plotted alongside a narrative explaining the baseline and counterfactual.
- **Pages 20–21:** scenario-to-history comparisons with consistent normalization.
- **Strength to adapt:** separate observed evidence, model assumptions and scenario outputs; show the *consequence* of a model as well as its input.
- **Avoid:** overloading SMPT decks with the density appropriate to a crisis briefing or reusing historic modeled values in a contemporary case.

**Rights:** The supplied McKinsey and BCG PDFs include copyright and/or express proprietary-use notices. Do not upload screenshot crops, downloaded figure images, logos or near-identical layouts to SMPT's public repository. The SVG examples under \`examples/\` are newly authored SMPT graphics.

### What these resources do *not* establish

- A universal professional slide count. SMPT's **20-slide ceiling is an owner-imposed editorial constraint**, not a rule stated by McKinsey or BCG.
- A universally preferred font. Georgia / Source Sans 3 was an earlier **SMPT choice**; the current presentation standard specifies Archivo.
- That consulting-branded slide screenshots may be redistributed in a public style guide.
- That a polished graphic substitutes for traceable assumptions or human editorial signoff.

## C. Principles adopted, ranked

1. **Analytical integrity** — no chart conceals financing status, dates, assumptions or missing evidence.
2. **Argument-led organization** — titles read as a coherent series of findings.
3. **Dominant and directly labeled exhibits** — quantitative comparisons deserve visual priority.
4. **Decision interpretation** — every important financial exhibit explains why its movement matters.
5. **Restrained, original visual system** — SMPT graphite/cream/gold, editorial typography, repeatable grids.
6. **Reproducible and rights-safe production** — editable deck, verified PDF, sources, models and original examples.

## D. How to extend these references

If a future SMPT report studies other consultant decks:
- Record exact document title, issuing organization, issue date, page number and what layout/argument is useful.
- Describe the principle in original words; do not paste screenshot crops.
- Check that a reference example's chosen chart actually suits the SMPT numerical question.
- Change the design specification only after reviewing at least one finished SMPT deck using the proposed pattern.
