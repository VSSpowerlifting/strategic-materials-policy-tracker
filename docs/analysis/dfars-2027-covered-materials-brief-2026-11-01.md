# US Defense Sourcing Restrictions on Covered Materials and Recorded US Public Capital — draft brief

**Status:** DRAFT, not human-reviewed. First drafted 2026-11-01; revised 2026-11-03 after source review (PR #185 review 5746985796). This is a proposed analytical brief. It is not an approved editorial conclusion, a legal opinion or a revised classification.

The legal instruments below are **not seed records**. They are described from primary text retrieved on 2026-11-02/03. Two related draft candidates sit in a private candidate queue and are unreviewed. Capital facts come only from existing seed records. Repository baseline: `main` at `b4834d75068459a0f60006735e9480df782fdaae`.

## Question

**Which recorded US public-financing instruments support supply-chain stages affected by defense materials sourcing restrictions? What legal requirements apply, and what remains unknown?**

The brief is for a reader who has to explain these restrictions to a defense contractor or a government-affairs team. It maps recorded financing against legal text. It does not estimate compliance, supply, capacity or cost. It does not claim that any financing was made because of a restriction.

## Short answer

- **Where the money sits.** Recorded US public commitments sit at the separation, refining and magnet-manufacturing stages of NdFeB magnets and rare earths. They are MP Materials (DoD), Vulcan Elements and ReElement (Office of Strategic Capital, OSC), and USA Rare Earth (CHIPS). Two 5N Plus grants sit at germanium refining.
- **Which stages the law tests.** Today the test is where a magnet or alloy is *melted or produced*. Contracts awarded on or after **1 January 2027** add a test of where covered material was *mined, refined or separated*.
- **Standing of the money.** Two rows are disbursed. One is partially disbursed. Several are contracted. The Vulcan/ReElement loans and the 2025 5N Plus grant are decided, not contracted.
- **What SMPT does not record.** SMPT records no US financing row for tantalum, tungsten or molybdenum. Each is covered by the statute.
- **What remains unknown.** Whether any financed output reaches a DoD contract. How "mined" applies to a magnet or an alloy. How the 2027 waiver limits will work in practice.

### 1. The statute: 10 U.S.C. 4872

**The prohibition.** Section 4872 bars the Department of Defense from procuring *covered material* "melted or produced" in a *covered nation*. It also bars an end item that contains such material and was manufactured in a covered nation (§4872(a)(1)–(2)). The bar reaches prime contracts and subcontracts at any tier (§4872(b)).

**Covered material** (§4872(f)(1)), in current text:
- (A) samarium-cobalt magnets;
- (B) neodymium-iron-boron magnets;
- (C) tungsten metal powder;
- (D) tungsten heavy alloy, or any finished or semi-finished component containing it;
- (E) tantalum metals and alloys;
- (F) molybdenum, added by Pub. L. 119-60 §844(a).

**Covered nations** (§4872(f)(2)): North Korea, China, Russia and Iran.

**Exceptions.** The exceptions in §4872(c) apply to (a)(1):
- an end item or material that cannot be procured at satisfactory quality and quantity, with a waiver of up to 36 months (§4872(c)(1));
- use outside the United States (§4872(c)(2));
- commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) items. This excludes items that are at least 50% tungsten by weight and excludes mill products (§4872(c)(3)(A));
- electronic devices (§4872(c)(3)(B));
- recycled NdFeB or samarium-cobalt magnets, milled and sintered in the US (§4872(c)(3)(C)). Pub. L. 119-60 §844(a) added samarium-cobalt;
- recycled tantalum, tungsten or molybdenum produced outside a covered nation and melted and processed in the US or a qualifying country (§4872(c)(3)(D)). This was added by §844(a).

A separate national security waiver lets DoD accept an end item that contains covered material (§4872(e)). Pub. L. 119-60 §843 lets a waiver under 10 U.S.C. 4863(k) also apply here (§4872(e)(3)).

**Amendments with later effective dates.** These are enacted but not yet in force:
- **1 January 2027.** Pub. L. 116-283 §844, as amended by Pub. L. 119-60 §848 (139 Stat. 978–979), adds a new paragraph (a)(3). It bars DoD from entering into "a contract for any covered material mined, refined, or separated in any covered nation". It also replaces "tungsten" with "covered material" in the COTS carve-out.
- **18 December 2027.** Pub. L. 119-60 §844(b) (139 Stat. 976) adds germanium and gallium as covered material. It changes the recycled-material exception to read "molybdenum, gallium, or germanium". This takes effect two years after enactment.

*In plain terms:* the statute already restricts where magnets and these metals are made. From 2027 it will also restrict where their raw inputs are mined and processed. Gallium and germanium join the list at the end of 2027.

### 2. The codified DFARS (MAY 2024)

The eCFR text, current to 2026-10-30, still shows DFARS 225.7018 and clause 252.225-7052 (MAY 2024). Sections 225.7018-2 and 252.225-7052 were last amended on 2024-05-30, and 225.7018-3 on 2024-08-15. This text:
- names five covered materials and omits molybdenum;
- states the 2027 test as "mined, refined, separated, melted, or produced".

No Federal Register rule implementing Pub. L. 119-60 has been located.

### 3. The class deviation now in use: 2026-O0041, Rev. 1

Under the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul, the Defense Pricing, Contracting, and Acquisition Policy (DPCAP) memo 2026-O0041 Rev. 1 directs contracting officers to use an attached DFARS Part 225 "in lieu of the text codified at 48 CFR chapter 2". The memo is signed by John M. Tenaglia and dated 1 July 2026 on the DPCAP deviation page. It revises the 3 February 2026 deviation and "implements section 848". It says the upstream restriction "applies only to contracts awarded on or after January 1, 2027". It remains in effect until rescinded or incorporated into the FAR, DFARS and PGI.

**Under the deviation:**
- **The two tests** (225.7018-2(a)). Contracting officers must not acquire covered material melted or produced in a covered country. They must also "not award a contract on or after January 1, 2027, for any covered material mined, refined, or separated in any covered country".
- **The clause** is **252.225-7961** (JUN 2026), which replaces 252.225-7052 in new solicitations.
  - Paragraph (b)(1)(i) bars delivery of melted or produced material.
  - Paragraph (b)(1)(ii) bars delivery "under a contract awarded on or after January 1, 2027" of material mined, refined or separated in a covered country.
  - Paragraphs (b)(2)–(b)(5) define the production stages that count for magnets, tantalum and tungsten.
- **Exceptions** (225.7018-3; clause (c)):
  - acquisitions at or below the simplified acquisition threshold (regulation only);
  - use outside the US;
  - COTS items. The carve-out excludes items at least 50% tungsten through 31 December 2026, items at least 50% covered material from 1 January 2027, and specified mill products;
  - electronic devices;
  - recycled NdFeB magnets milled and sintered in the US;
  - a nonavailability determination under 225.7018-4. The head of the contracting activity makes an individual determination; USD(A&S) makes a class determination.
- **Flowdown** (clause (d)). Contractors must insert the clause's substance in subcontracts for items containing covered material, including commercial products, unless an exception applies.

**Where the deviation lags the statute.**
- It still lists five covered materials. **Molybdenum is not in the clause**, although it is in the statute.
- The clause's recycled exception covers only NdFeB. It omits the statutory samarium-cobalt and tantalum/tungsten/molybdenum recycled exceptions.
- The deviation's nonavailability template still cites 252.225-7052.

DFARS case **2024-D014** ("Exception for Restriction on Certain Metal Products") is the open rulemaking for Pub. L. 119-60 §§843–844. The DFARS open-cases report, status as of 30 October 2026, says the scope was revised "to remove section 848", because Rev. 1 implements it. A report is due 18 November 2026. No proposed or final rule has been located.

*In plain terms:* contracting officers now work from the deviation, not the 2024 regulation. The deviation puts the 2027 upstream test in place for new contracts. It has not yet added molybdenum or the newer recycled-material exceptions. A separate rulemaking is expected to do that.

### 4. Executive Order 14415 (20 July 2026)

The order is "Securing America's Defense Supply Chains and Ensuring Domestic Acquisition of Critical Materials", 91 FR 46693, FR Doc. 2026-15003, published 23 July 2026. It directs agencies; it does not amend the statute or the DFARS. **It neither enacts nor postpones the statutory 2027 test.**

- **§2(a), waivers.** "On January 1, 2027", the Secretary of War and the Secretaries of the military departments "shall cease to issue waivers under 10 U.S.C. 4872(c)(1)", except under §2(b). Waivers under §4872(e) issue only under §2(b) or after a request to the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs.
- **§2(b), mitigation-plan exception.** A waiver may still issue if the contractor submits, and the Secretary accepts, a formal mitigation plan. The plan must identify the source, show exhaustive efforts or unavailability, give removal steps and set a strict timeline.
- **§2(c).** Failing to qualify a domestic source is not non-availability unless qualification efforts are active, funded and ongoing.
- **§2(e)–(f).** A list of remedies is due within 180 days (about 16 January 2027). The order also directs a review of the electronic-device exception.
- **§3, supply-chain mapping.** Policy and guidance are due within 180 days, with implementing regulations to follow. They will require prime contractors and subcontractors at any tier to map critical supply chains. This includes an indentured bill of materials traced to raw-material origin, supplier vetting and a bar on covered material from an "unreliable foreign supplier" (§7(f)).
  - **Implementation status:** no §3 guidance or regulation has been located as of 2026-11-03. Its deadline has not yet passed.
- **§4(c).** A strategy for qualifying sources was due within 90 days (about 18 October 2026). **No published strategy has been located.**
- **§6(c).** Nothing in the order "shall be construed to impair or otherwise affect" a contractor's acquisition from a company or project receiving support from the Departments of State, War, Commerce or Energy. **This limits the order only. It does not exempt anyone from 10 U.S.C. 4872 or the DFARS clause.**

*In plain terms:* from 2027 the executive branch intends to make §4872(c)(1) waivers rare and conditional on a mitigation plan. The order's own new requirements will not be applied against suppliers financed by these departments. The statutory restriction still applies to them.

### 5. H.R. 9073 (pending)

H.R. 9073 was introduced on 29 May 2026 and referred to the House Armed Services Committee. Congress.gov still lists that referral as its latest action (checked 2026-11-03). The bill would apply the upstream test only to contracts entered into on or after the **earlier** of 1 January 2032 or 180 days after a Secretary of Defense certification. **It is a proposal, not law.** The 1 January 2027 date stands unless Congress acts.

## Method and counting boundary

- **Scope.** The brief reads five existing seed events and their `fin-*` rows. All are US federal actions on rare earths, magnets or germanium, recorded from 2024 to 2026. Antimony rows are left out because antimony is not covered material under §4872, before or after Pub. L. 119-60.
- **Status.** Each row shows its `amountAsStated` (the source's own words) and the **last entry** of its financial status history, which is the current status. "Date not stated" means the seed records the status without a source-given date. The status ladder is:
  - *announced* or *indication*: a statement of intent;
  - *decided*: an agency decision, such as a conditional commitment;
  - *contracted*: a signed agreement;
  - *disbursed* or *partially disbursed*: money paid.
- **No totals.** Nothing is summed: not within an event, not across instruments and not across value roles.
- **Parts are not additive.** A row marked *part of* is an allocation of its parent and is never added to it. A row marked *drawn from* is funded from the named authority. It is not a second commitment.
- **Separation of money types.** The tables put public commitments first, then options and indications, then private rows. Private rows are shown so the record is complete. They are not public support.
- **Stage and material** are the tracker's codes (`stages`, `materialIds`). They describe what the financed activity covers. They do not say whether any output will be sold to DoD or will meet the clause. A row coded at a stage the law examines *sits at* that stage. It does not "fall under" the restriction, which binds DoD procurement and the contracts that flow from it.

## Analysis (draft; alignment, not causation)

This section maps recorded stages and materials against the legal text. It makes no claim about why any financing was made.

1. **Upstream magnet feedstock (2027 test).** The deviation and the statute test where covered material was mined, refined or separated, for contracts awarded on or after 1 January 2027. Several rows are coded at separation or refining for rare earths:
   - the MP preferred equity and the MP samarium loan;
   - the Vulcan/ReElement joint commitment (its two part loans carry no stage codes of their own);
   - the USA Rare Earth awards, which are coded from mining through component manufacturing.

   These rows sit at stages the 2027 test examines. Their legal standing differs:
   - **Disbursed:** MP equity and MP samarium loan.
   - **Contracted:** USA Rare Earth direct funding and loan guarantee.
   - **Decided, conditional, not contracted:** Vulcan and ReElement loans.

   It is an open legal question how "mined" applies to a magnet, which is not itself mined. The text does not say whether the test follows the rare-earth feedstock.
2. **Magnet manufacture (current test).** The test in force today asks where a magnet is "melted or produced". Clause 252.225-7961(b)(2) counts melting and every later production stage. Public rows coded at component manufacturing therefore sit at a stage the current test already examines:
   - the MP preferred equity and the MP offtake;
   - the Vulcan/ReElement joint commitment;
   - the USA Rare Earth awards and their magnet allocations.

   MP's own cash is also coded there, but it is private money. The recycled-magnet exception is a separate route, and no row in this set is coded to it.
3. **Waiver environment.** If EO 14415 §2 is carried out as written, §4872(c)(1) waivers become exceptional from 1 January 2027 and depend on an accepted mitigation plan. That raises the stakes of a non-covered-nation source. It does not show that any recorded financing was planned for that purpose. EO §6(c) means the order's own new restrictions will not be applied against suppliers that receive this support. The statute still applies to them.
4. **Germanium and gallium timing.** Gallium and germanium become covered material only on 18 December 2027. The two 5N Plus grants and the USA Rare Earth award's gallium coding come before that date. Shared materials and dates do not show that the grants respond to the amendment.
5. **Outside the frame.** Tantalum, tungsten and molybdenum are covered material, but SMPT tracks no US financing row for them. Samarium, tantalum and molybdenum have no material ID in the seed. This reflects SMPT's coverage, not evidence that no such financing exists.

## What remains unknown

- **DoD use.** Whether any financed output enters a DoD prime contract or subcontract. SMPT records no procurement data.
- **Meaning of "mined".** How "mined, refined, or separated" applies to magnets and alloys. Whether the test traces each feedstock input, and how far down the tiers it reaches.
- **Scope of the exceptions.** Statute §4872(c) says its exceptions apply to (a)(1). The new (a)(3) is not named. The deviation's clause applies its exceptions to the whole clause. How this resolves is not stated in any located source.
- **Regulatory lag.** When DFARS case 2024-D014 will add molybdenum and the newer recycled exceptions, and in what form.
- **Executive order deliverables.** Whether the EO 14415 §3 guidance, §2(e) remedies and §4(c) strategy are issued, and what they say. None was located as of 2026-11-03.
- **Waiver practice.** How many §4872 waivers were issued before 2027 and how many are issued after. No source was located.
- **Pending legislation.** Whether H.R. 9073 or a similar provision in a later NDAA moves.
