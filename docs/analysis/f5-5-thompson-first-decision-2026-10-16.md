### What we independently read in the original SEC filing

**Registered primary source** `src-usac-2026-q2-10q`, [USAC 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101538/000110465926094035/uamy-20260630x10q.htm). The issuer signed/filed the report on **August 11, 2026**, for the period ended **June 30, 2026**. Source registry `dateAccessed` is **October 2, 2026** and remains unchanged. These are three **different** clocks.

- [**Note 16 — Commitments and Contingencies, “Thompson Falls, Montana Facility Expansion”**](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101538/000110465926094035/R22.htm) puts the issuer's *substantially completed* construction statement **during Q2**, while reporting approximately **US$39M planned expansion capital expenditures**, **US$33M incurred by June 30**, about **US$4M transferred to property, plant and equipment**, and approximately **US$29M of gross project costs** remaining in construction in progress. A “substantially completed” phrase is narrower than every asset line placed in service or zero CIP.
- [**Note 9 — Government Grant**](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101538/000110465926094035/R15.htm) separately describes approximately **US$4.1M of related assets placed in service late Q2**. Note 9 reports the **US$27M DPA award**, consisting of **US$16.2M currently obligated** and **US$10.8M subject to future authorization**. It also describes formal **DIBC acceptance of three funding-performance milestones on March 25** associated with **US$12.8M recognized as a grant receivable and collected in April**. **Those are contractual funding-performance milestones, not three separately observed furnace commissioning events.**
- [**August 11 SEC-furnished earnings Exhibit 99.1**](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101538/000110465926094130/tm2622899d1_ex99-1.htm) still **forecast** full operation of all new furnaces in Q3. Issuer forward guidance is a different assertion from past Q2 construction progress.
- [**August 27 original Sanders County Ledger on-site report**](https://www.scledger.net/story/2026/08/27/news/usac-capacity-expands-with-new-t-falls-facility/14209.html), by Annie Wooden, offers genuinely independent original reporting of activity at the **new** plant. It describes **seven of nine *new* furnaces operational but not all concurrently**. The **old** smelter contains another seven furnaces. Reporter publication day is not a verified physical first-operation day; no permission to republish original photos or article text is implied. This independent article is still only an *unregistered* research lead, not a new canonical M3 `sourceId`.
- [**October 2, 2025 original Ledger groundbreaking report**](https://www.scledger.net/story/2025/10/02/news/usac-celebrates-expansion/12856.html) says the construction **began in May 2025** and separately describes the **late September** celebration. Both are prior to the government grant funded in 2026. Neither supplies a verified *day* of original construction commencement. Funding cannot retroactively be labeled the *cause* of work that preceded it.

### Independent government engineering-scope evidence — Montana DEQ original permit

**New independent original: [Montana DEQ MAQP #2973-05, final and effective December 23, 2025](https://deq.mt.gov/files/Air/AirQuality/Documents/ARMpermits/2973-05_FNL.pdf)**, 77-page original regulator-issued permit and analysis for U.S. Antimony's Thompson Falls plant (legal location Section 29, Township 21 North, Range 31 West, Sanders County). [DEQ's public permit registry](https://deq.mt.gov/air/assistance) lists USAC, #2973-05, and the December 23, 2025 effective date. This original regulator record provides an **independent documentary source for *permitted industrial configuration***. It is **not an inspection report, source-test result, certificate of commissioned status, proof of operating rate, or new independent operating milestone**. It has **not** been added to the canonical source registry, and there is no archived source-body checksum in this phase.

**Precise source locators and permitted equipment (PDF page numbers below refer to physical PDF index, starting from 0):**

- **PDF p. 0, state cover letter; p. 2, Section I.B — permit action**: initial company application received **September 3, 2025**, application complete **October 7**, preliminary determination **November 10**, decision **December 5**, permit **final December 23, 2025**. These are regulatory review clocks, **not industrial construction/commissioning days**. The requested expansion adds **five new reduction, two new remelt, and two new oxidizer furnaces**, all with **3.0 MMBtu/hour input ratings**, plus associated baghouses/conveyors; changes control equipment on **six previously permitted** units (four reduction, one remelt, one oxidizer, each **0.85 MMBtu/hour**).
- **PDF p. 7, Permit Analysis §I.A; p. 9, response to applicant comments**: DEQ identifies **nine new** versus **six existing permitted** units and specifically records that the applicant identified an earlier mistake in the new-versus-old furnace rating/count; DEQ states it corrected the permit. A 3.0 MMBtu/hour **thermal-input rating** is not metal tonnes per hour and does not prove realized processing output, nor does inclusion in a regulatory equipment schedule prove actual startup.
- **PDF p. 4, §II.B.1 testing**: DEQ requires the specified initial EPA emissions-source tests after initial startup of **all new** reduction/remelt/oxidizer furnaces, with a permit-defined window, and subsequent periodic checks. **No completed test report or site-inspection results are included in this issued permit.** Do not infer that a test was performed—or that its deadline has arrived—without verifying the startup date and actual DEQ compliance record.
- **PDF pp. 4–5, §II.C operational reporting and records**: annual calendar-year production/emissions inventory submissions and records available to DEQ upon request could eventually help independently assess operations. The presence of a **reporting duty** is not proof an inventory, source-test or inspection evidencing 2026 commissioning is publicly available.

**Unresolved independent-source discrepancy:** The December 2025 DEQ permit schedules **six older permitted furnaces**, while the **August 27, 2026 Ledger** describes **seven furnaces in the old smelter building**. Do not silently harmonize those counts or state that either source is necessarily wrong or a permit was violated. They may describe different dates or definitions of operating, physical, and permit-controlled equipment. Human review should check furnace IDs and subsequent permitting changes. Both sources agree that the **new** building has *nine* furnace units; the local reporter independently says **seven of those nine were operating, not simultaneously** by the report date.

**Concrete remaining primary-document research:** If independently available, obtain DEQ's actual **startup notices**, **initial stack-test reports/protocols**, **site inspections**, subsequent **permit modifications**, and **production/emissions inventory data**, specifically for **MAQP #2973-05** and the **nine new** furnace units for May–October 16, 2026. [Montana DEQ's public-records access page](https://www.deq.mt.gov/about/public-records) is the official discovery/request route. This is a proposed research request only: **no record request has been sent and none of those reports has been found or authenticated**. Negative search results must never be interpreted as evidence that no furnace operated.

The [machine-readable DEQ research packet](../../research/f5/thompson-deq-2973-05-permit-review.json) captures those distinct original claims, exact page/section locators, test-report evidence gaps, nine-versus-six permitting identity, and six-versus-seven older-furnace discrepancy.
